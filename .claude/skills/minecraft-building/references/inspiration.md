# Reference builds: what high-quality looks like

The user shared these as the quality bar. Each entry says what the build does well and how to
reproduce it with blocks. Read this before any build players will spend time in. If image files
exist in `references/images/`, look at them too.

## 1. Underground command hall (facility interior)

A huge, tall hall full of workstations under a busy ceiling. Why it works:

- **Scale and height.** The room is tall enough (12+ blocks) that the ceiling becomes its own
  layer of detail. Halls feel important when the ceiling is far away.
- **An engineered ceiling.** Exposed beams (stone-brick slabs and stairs running in parallel lines),
  ducting, iron-bar grates, and **hanging light boxes**: glowing blocks (ochre/verdant froglight,
  sea lantern, glowstone) wrapped in a frame of copper grates, scaffolding or trapdoors, hung on chains
  at varying heights. Lights are objects, not just glowing blocks in a flat ceiling.
- **Walls with depth.** Pilasters protrude one block every 3–4 blocks; panels between them are
  recessed and mixed (stone bricks, tuff bricks, andesite, calcite, cracked variants). A **gallery
  catwalk** runs along an upper wall with railings (iron bars, glass panes), and a staircase climbs to it.
- **One accent color, used consistently.** Yellow (bamboo blocks, hay, yellow concrete or yellow
  glazed terracotta) trims tables, door frames and shelving. Everything else is cool gray-blue,
  so the yellow reads as "this facility's design language".
- **A floor that guides.** A warm smooth-sandstone floor, with a black-and-white checker walkway
  (black, gray and white concrete) leading the eye to the big screen at the far end.
- **Furniture built from several blocks** (see the micro-builds below): workstations of quartz or
  white concrete with a glass inset and yellow trim, monitors on them, chairs, tall shelving units,
  floor lamps.
- **Screens and signals.** A huge black-concrete display wall, red status lights (redstone lamps
  or red glass) beside doors.

## 2. Canyon outpost (exterior on terrain)

A concrete research outpost built into a terracotta canyon. Why it works:

- **A strong vertical.** A tall watchtower beside a squat bunker body. Contrast in height makes
  the silhouette readable from far away.
- **A dark frame around light walls.** Deepslate or blackstone pillars and slab ledges outline a
  body of smooth stone, calcite or white concrete, like steel scaffolding around concrete.
- **Weathering by color.** Patches of orange and brown terracotta on the concrete read as rust
  and water stains. Do it in streaks under ledges, never as random noise.
- **Hazard detail.** Yellow-and-black railings and stripes on walkways and stairs.
- **Exterior stairs and catwalks** that wrap the building and step down the terrain.
- **Antennas and gear.** Lightning rods or end rods with a red tip (redstone torch) on masts, AC
  units and dishes from heads, trapdoors and slabs, crates on the walkways.
- **Layered roof overhangs.** Several thin slab layers stepping out, so the top has a shadow line.
- **It sits in the land.** Foundations follow the slope, dead bushes and grass at its feet, a
  little vegetation on the ledges.

## 3. Overgrown facility atrium (interior reclaimed by nature)

A clean facility interior where a huge tree grew up through the floor. Why it works:

- **A centerpiece that tells the story.** A tree (dark oak or jungle logs, leaves, roots) rising
  through a broken floor and spreading under the ceiling says "years passed" instantly.
- **Order vs. chaos.** Pale, clean architecture (calcite, smooth stone, quartz) with low walls and
  railings, against dark organic shapes. The contrast is the point.
- **Hazard tape** (yellow and black concrete or banners) around the breach, as if someone tried to
  contain it.
- **Hanging growth.** Vines and glow lichen from the ceiling and railings, moss creeping across
  the tiles, cracked tiles near the roots.

## 4. Overgrown city street (exterior ruins)

A street of mid-rise buildings decades after the end. Why it works:

- **Buildings with identity.** Each facade has its own color (purple and terracotta walls, red
  brick, white trim bands) and a regular window grid. Ruins read best when the original building
  is still recognizable.
- **Nature on every horizontal surface.** Moss blocks, leaves (azalea, oak, flowering azalea) and
  grass blanket roofs, ledges and the tops of walls. Vertical surfaces get vines and glow lichen
  in falling streaks.
- **A broken street.** Cobbled deepslate, gravel and coarse dirt patches, moss carpet in the
  cracks, grass and ferns pushing up, with stair and slab curbs.
- **Street furniture.** Abandoned cars (built from blocks), street lights (fence posts with a
  lantern or end rod), benches (stairs with trapdoor arms), planters, a billboard on a wall.
- **Trees growing out of buildings.** Through roofs, out of windows, in the middle of a plaza.

## 5. Overgrown skyline (large-scale landmarks)

A tower, a domed hall and a suspension bridge, all reclaimed. Why it works:

- **Repetition at scale.** A tower's window grid repeated many stories. The eye accepts a lot of
  repetition if the material changes in bands.
- **Facades with relief.** Cornices, columns and arches made from stairs, slabs and walls, so the
  surface has shadow at every scale.
- **Copper and oxidization.** A dome in oxidized copper or prismarine; green-blue against gray stone.
- **Cascading greenery.** Vines and leaves pour down from the top in long streaks, heavier near the
  top and on the shaded side.
- **A bridge** with chain cables and towers, hung with vines, over water. Water and reflections
  sell the scene.

## Micro-builds (reusable furniture and props)

| Prop | Recipe |
| --- | --- |
| Chair | stair facing the table, with trapdoors on both sides as arms |
| Office chair | stair on a fence post, or a stair with a trapdoor back |
| Desk / workstation | slab tops (quartz, white concrete or smooth stone) on a frame; a glass-pane or tinted-glass inset; a yellow trim block at the ends |
| Monitor | a player head or a block with a trapdoor or painting face; or black stained glass with a cyan or green pixel |
| Floor lamp | end rod or fence column with a white wool or quartz slab shade over a light block |
| Hanging light box | froglight or sea lantern inside copper grates or scaffolding, hung by chains |
| Shelving unit | scaffolding or bamboo frame with slabs as shelves, barrels and chests on them |
| Status light | redstone lamp (lit, powered from behind) or red glass with a light behind |
| Railing | iron bars, glass panes, or yellow-and-black trapdoors on a catwalk edge |
| Antenna | lightning rods stacked, with a redstone torch or end rod tip |
| AC unit / machinery | heads, trapdoors and grindstones on a wall; lightning rods as pipes |
| Crate | barrels, bamboo blocks or scaffolding stacked |
| Car (ruined) | 5x2 base of stairs and slabs in a color, trapdoor wheels, glass-pane windscreen, moss carpet on top |
| Street light | fence or wall column, a lantern or end rod hanging from a trapdoor arm |
| Bench | stairs in a row with trapdoors at the ends |
| Billboard | a flat wall of concrete with a simple pixel image, on fence legs |
