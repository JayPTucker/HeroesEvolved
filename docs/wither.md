# The Wither: Containment Failure

**One idea: a broken cage around a hungry star.** Its heads, ribs, attacks and arena are all drawn toward the Nether
Star inside its chest, and everything it does is a tear in the world or the hunger pulling at one. Its palette is
charred and pale bone, cold mint light, white, and the violet-black depth of a breach.

## Well, That's Not Normal...

Build the familiar four-block soul-sand or soul-soil body and place the three Wither skulls. The Wither never gets
loose. It knits together where you built it, turns, and roars at you. Behind it a hairline seam of light splits the air,
cracks race out from it, and the seam tears open into a breach: a jagged mint-and-white rim round a black depth that
falls away like a tunnel, star specks drifting down it and shards of broken space circling it. The breach drags at the
Wither. It digs its claws into the ground and screams at you while its tail is drawn in, and then it is yanked
backwards through, arms flung toward you, swallowed into the dark. The breach convulses and shrinks to the size of a
door, and stays. There is no explosion at the summoning site. Nearby witnesses earn **Well, That's Not Normal...**

Walk into the breach to enter. You need an awakened ability at **Power Level 16 or higher**. A lone player can fight;
friends enter separately and can join late. Only one Wither encounter is open at a time; another construction while
it is open returns its ingredients.

**The return breach stays open throughout the encounter.** It waits in an alcove behind the Ossuary's wall. Retreat
through it whenever you need to; damage from that shelter cannot hurt the boss. If everybody retreats, the Wither
recovers and the abandoned attempt resets; the summoning-site breach remains for another try. Health grows with
additional fighters and never shrinks during an attempt. A server restart returns fighters safely and leaves the
entrance ready. Death drops are kept for their owner and returned on respawn.

## The Ossuary

A round blackstone floor **59 blocks across**, walled by a charnel ring ten blocks high: basalt pilasters, and between
them niches of heaped bones with skulls watching the floor, some lit by pale green froglight. Over it all stands a
vault of **seven giant fossil ribs on one spine**, as if the fight happens inside the ribcage of something far bigger
than the Wither; soul lanterns hang on chains from its knuckles, and a great fossil skull stares down the floor from
above the entrance arch. Six dark channels run from a star-shaped dais to the wall; they burn violet in the second
phase and ember in the third. Six broken pillars are the cover, and three bone pylons are the
counterplay: in each, a **Hunger Heart** (a crystal of the star's light) floats in an open cradle two blocks tall,
open on all four sides. It is a separate dimension, rebuilt before each attempt.

There are **44 blocks of usable flight height** under the vault's crown. The Wither rises toward flying opponents, and
its marked lanes, skulls and star discharge reach the air. Crossing the outer bounds returns you to the entrance.

**It arrives.** When the first fighter steps in, a tear opens high over the dais, and the Wither falls out of it, lands
in a ring of thrown-up floor, and roars at the room before the first chain. It cannot be hurt until it has landed and
roared. It comes **warded**: a lattice of mint light over its ribs, fed by a thread from each heart.

## Your power is how you win

It is not beaten with a sword and armour. Every phase puts two or three **locks** in front of you, and every one of the
twenty-one powers opens at least one of them well in every phase. Plain weapons (blades, arrows, bullets) are the slow
answer anyone has: they do half to a heart and 60% to its body, while a power's blows (a fighting style's fists
included) do it all, and a power whose answer is its blows does two and a half times as much (or nearly twice) to the
lock. Some powers answer a lock outright instead: **look straight at it and use your power** (any move of 8 stamina or
more) and a strong answer takes two fifths of it at once (a weaker one a quarter). A turning diamond of light shows,
only to you, every lock your power answers: brighter for your best answers. The Guide's **The Hungry Cage** topic lists
what your power does in each phase.

| Phase | Its locks |
| --- | --- |
| **Threefold Hunger** | **The Star Ward.** While three hearts feed it, it takes a quarter of every blow (two hearts a half, one three quarters) and cannot be brought below seven tenths of its health. A broken heart re-forms in thirty seconds while the ward stands; break all three and the ward shatters off its ribs and throws it down (3 s, chest open). **Its skulls.** Any skull sent back (a punch works; Wind Arms, Bullet Catch, a force field, a quake's push, a sonic wall or stopped time do it better) shatters a heart outright and hits the Wither for a twenty-fifth of its health, past the ward. While its ward stands, a side head turns on whoever goes for a heart. |
| **The Broken Vessel** | **Its mending brood.** A skeleton that has stood seven seconds walks back to it carrying bone (a pale thread to its star); one that reaches it climbs into its cage and mends 3% of its health and a crack in its stance. Clear them, hold them or turn them first. **A feeding heart.** The hearts re-form asleep; when it winds up a heavy move the sleeping heart nearest its focus lights and drinks for seven seconds. Break it and its hunger backfires: 8% of its health, its chain cancelled, reeling. Each heart backfires once a phase. **The Hunger Mark.** Its bite and its hook leave a violet thread to its star: while it can see you it drinks a point of health a second from you and heals itself. Put stone between you for three seconds, drink milk, or let a cleansing power lift it. |
| **Critical Mass** | **Event Horizon.** Cover still saves you. A shield power holds it; a power used as the white ring closes on the star carries a dodging or time-stopping power out of it; a regenerator outlasts it. **Its open star.** A shot that lands on the star, a blow from up at its height or one aimed straight at it does 60% more (a power whose answer is its blows, its full answer); three in quick succession make it reel. **The hearts** re-form and feed again. |

Nothing heals it back into a phase you have beaten.

## Three phases

| Phase | Health | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Threefold Hunger** | 100-67% | Mint eyes and star. It pursues and circles, linking bites, claw rakes, beams, skull rain, floor pulses, tethers and its first brood of skeletons in short chains, under its Star Ward until its three hearts are broken. |
| **Broken Vessel** | 67-33% | A roar throws everyone back; its armoured pauldrons crash to the floor, fractures light through its bones in violet, the channels burn violet, and skeletal flankers climb out at the wall. In the arena it stands about **ten blocks tall**: a skeletal sovereign with three heads on stretching necks, long clawed
arms, a hinged ribcage round the star and a dragging tail. Its whole body reads every move before it lands, and every
hit is also marked on the floor or in the air at its real size. The mark is fixed when the windup starts: step off it.

Side heads sometimes prepare a separate skull during another move: a mouth light and a fixed aiming thread warn first.
Its attention rotates between fighters in a party.

**Its brood.** Every so often it spews wither skeletons out of its mouth: two at a time in the first phase, three
later, one more for every two extra fighters. Each flies a smoking arc onto its marked circle beside a fighter, hurts
anyone standing there, and fights. No more than three plus one per fighter stand at once (eight at most), and it waits
30, 22 and then 18 seconds between broods. They are its own bones: they crumble to dust after thirty seconds or when it
dies, drop nothing and give no experience, so there is nothing to farm. The skeletons that climb out at the wall when
the vessel breaks are the same brood.

## How hard it is

It is built to be won by reading it and answering it with your power, not by tanking it (the bar is Cataclysm's bosses
and Bosses'Rise's Souls-like ones), and it assumes you have strong gear from other mods. It has **720 health** (more for
a party; each heart has 50, more for a party). **No weapon carries the fight:** no blow takes more than 5% of its
health and no second more than 2.5%, so even a flawless party with the strongest weapons needs forty seconds of clean
damage, and its ward refuses anything below seven tenths until its hearts are broken. **No armour makes you safe:** 30%
of every blow it lands is its star's hunger, which goes through any armour, and its heavy blows (the slam, the charge,
the tail, the Breach Step's arrival, a well's collapse and the discharge) also take 5% of your own maximum health, so
extra hearts from other mods buy time, not safety.
Every move holds an opening pose long enough to read (at least 8 ticks for the bite, 10 for the hook, 12 for anything
with a mark on the floor), strikes, and holds its end pose for a moment before its window opens.

| What | First phase | Broken Vessel | Critical Mass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Windups | bite 0.5 s, rake and hook 0.7 s, others 0.8 to 1 s, discharge 2.4 s | about 14% faster | about 26% faster (never under its reading time) |
| Chains | three moves | three or four | three or four, and every third is the discharge |
| Rest between moves | 60% of the move's | 45% | a third |
| Slump after a chain | 1.5 s | 1.1 s | 0.8 s |
| Side-head skulls | every 3 s | every 2.2 s | every 1.6 s |
| Damage (before armour) | bite 11, rake 13, beam 9, skull 8, floor ring 9, hook 7, charge 14, slam 16, spire 9, brood landing 6, Breach Step 10, tail 12, cage 6, well 14, discharge 22 | +15% | +30% |

It moves faster too: it closes distance at a run, turns quicker, and when you are far away it doesn't walk to you, it
steps through a breach and comes out behind you.

Every hit also withers you (Wither II for four seconds). **Feints:** from the second phase every third bite, rake,
slam, charge and tail holds its coil half again as long. The mark still fills exactly as the hit lands, so dodge when the mark
completes, not when the body moves. **Combos:** from the second phase, whoever Grave Tether hauls in is bitten as they
land; in Critical Mass a charge that misses wheels round and comes again at once (bait the second into stone too).
Its last ring pulses twice in the second phase and three times in the third. **Starving:** in its last 15% it winds up
about 12% faster again, rests less and hits 10% harder.

Its bar is its own: a bone shaft with rib teeth and knuckled ends, its three skulls over the middle, the fill and their
eyes burning mint, violet or ember with the phase, and a pale trail showing the health just lost.

## Turn its hunger against it

| Window | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Rhythm** | After a chain it slumps with its chest open and takes **35% more** damage, briefly (see above). Slams leave it pulling its fists from the floor. |
| **Skill** | **Three spaced heavy hits** during its windups crack its stance (three marks round the star) and break the chain. Fully charged melee strikes, critical arrows, returned skulls and single hits of at least eight count. |
| **Room** | Bait **Ribbreaker** into a pillar: the warning shows a burst where the lane meets stone; it crashes, is stunned for 2.5 s and takes 35% more. Phase finishers force the next phase with a long stagger; in the final tenth they can finish the Wither.

## Its death

The killing blow does not end it. It thrashes, light cracking across its bones; its ribs burst outward in a spray of
bone; it rises over the dais with its arms thrown wide and its heads screaming at the vault while the star swells and
beams break out through every fracture. Then the star drinks everything back in, smoke and light spiralling into it,
the room pulled toward it, and goes off.

| The blast | |
| --- | --- |
| **No shield** | Thrown off your feet and flat on your back. A white flash, out cold for two and a half seconds with your ears ringing and the world muffled, then your eyes blink open on the vault before you can get up, about seven and a half seconds in all. |
| **A shield raised toward it** | You slide back, still standing. |
| **A shield-making power** (Invisibility, Telekinesis, Matter Assembly, Cryokinesis, Sonic Resonance) | Your shield flares and holds; you stay standing. |

The blast does no damage: it ends the fight, it doesn't end you. While everyone is still on the ground, the air tears
open **where it died**: the way out, a breach like the one at home, and its prizes are thrown out of it toward the
fighters who earned them. Walk into it to go home (anything of yours still on the floor goes with you). The Ossuary
closes once everyone has left, or two minutes after the victory, sending anyone left home with their prizes; the breach
at the summoning site slams shut.

**Prizes.** A Nether Star for the killing blow, a Fractured Star for every participant, containment notes pointing
toward the powered End portal for first-time winners. A Fractured Star can be crafted into a Breach Seed for another
attempt. Later victories have a one-in-ten chance of the theme's record.

## Music: The Hungry Cage

An original score in A minor on a 75 bpm pulse driven in sixteenths, with a Phrygian B-flat always pulling at the A
(the hunger is a half step). A relentless low pulse runs under everything; its heartbeat is three big drum hits and a
gap, one for each head; its kit is bone rattles and chain clanks; its horn call climbs to a half step at the top and
falls home. Every loop breathes the same way: a hush, an impact, a build, two sudden full stops that slam back in, and a
rise back round. **Threefold Hunger** is the pulse, the heartbeat, the bones, low strings from the first impact and
the call in the low horns; **Broken Vessel** adds the full kit, a spiccato ostinato, horns and stabs, a low choir, and
at each turn an impact that leaves a pulsing low drone; **Critical Mass** doubles the drums, puts the call in the high
brass and the choir, and adds a high string tremolo and a rising sub into each turn. Phase changes play a stinger (one
bright hit on a suspended chord, rattles and chains tumbling off it) and cross to the next loop on the next bar.

When it dies the fight's music stops; after the detonation its last chord rises out of the ringing (the heartbeat
failing, a bell, A minor opening to a bare fifth), and as that dies away **the aftermath** fades in: slow blocks of low
chords (A minor, B-flat, F, D minor) struck bar by bar under a low choir and a far bell, looping while the way out
stands open and fading when you leave. It follows the Music slider and is heard only inside the Ossuary.

The user picked the references in their Gemini: Heroes Reborn library: two Music_06 layers for the fight, a third for
after it dies, and a Score 01 stinger. They set its key, tempo, length, loudness, spectrum, density and shape; none of
their recordings or melodies is in the mod.

## Guide and admin

The in-game Guide's **The Hungry Cage** topic covers the breach, reading its body, its locks (the ward and its hearts,
returned skulls, the mending brood, the Hunger Mark, the feeding hearts, the open star and the discharge's answers), its
windows, the death blast and the way out, and ends with what the reader's own power does to it in each phase. Entering
the Ossuary, the ward shattering and each phase change say one line in chat. Operators can select **Wither: Containment
Failure** in the admin console's Story tab to open a breach near the selected player, enter an eligible player, advance
the live phase, **finish it** (straight to the death scene, the detonation and the exit), **break its ward** or cancel
the encounter. Live status names the ward and how many hearts feed it, the spent and feeding hearts, marked fighters,
the brood, the current attack, stance, health, fighters, the death scene and the exit's countdown. Phase changes, finishing and cancellation affect the whole
encounter; cancellation returns fighters and also stops a queued formation.
