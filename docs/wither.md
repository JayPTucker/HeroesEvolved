# The Wither: Containment Failure

**One idea: a broken cage around a hungry star.** Its heads, ribs, attacks and arena are all drawn toward the Nether
Star inside its chest, and everything it does is a tear in the world or the hunger pulling at one. Its palette is
charred and pale bone, cold mint light, white, and the violet-black depth of a breach.

## Well, That's Not Normal...

Build the familiar four-block soul-sand or soul-soil body and place the three Wither skulls. The Wither never gets
loose. It knits together where you built it, turns, and roars at you. Behind it a hairline seam of light splits the air,
cracks race out from it, and the seam tears open into a breach: a jagged mint-and-white rim round a black depth that
falls away like a tunnel, star specks drifting down it and shards of broken space circling it. The breach drags at the
Wither. It digs its claws into the ground and screams at you while its tail is drawn in, and then it is yanked
backwards through, arms flung toward you, swallowed into the dark. The breach convulses and shrinks to the size of a
door, and stays. There is no explosion at the summoning site. Nearby witnesses earn **Well, That's Not Normal...**

Walk into the breach to enter. You need an awakened ability at **Power Level 16 or higher**. A lone player can fight;
friends enter separately and can join late. Only one Wither encounter is open at a time; another construction while
it is open returns its ingredients.

**The return breach stays open throughout the encounter.** It waits in an alcove behind the Ossuary's wall. Retreat
through it whenever you need to; damage from that shelter cannot hurt the boss. If everybody retreats, the Wither
recovers and the abandoned attempt resets; the summoning-site breach remains for another try. Health grows with
additional fighters and never shrinks during an attempt. A server restart returns fighters safely and leaves the
entrance ready. Death drops are kept for their owner and returned on respawn.

## The Ossuary

A round blackstone floor **59 blocks across**, walled by a charnel ring ten blocks high: basalt pilasters, and between
them niches of heaped bones with skulls watching the floor, some lit by pale green froglight. Over it all stands a
vault of **seven giant fossil ribs on one spine**, as if the fight happens inside the ribcage of something far bigger
than the Wither; soul lanterns hang on chains from its knuckles, and a great fossil skull stares down the floor from
above the entrance arch. Six dark channels run from a star-shaped dais to the wall; they burn violet in the second
phase and ember in the third. Six broken pillars are the cover, and three bone-cradled anchor pylons are the
counterplay. It is a separate dimension, rebuilt before each attempt.

There are **44 blocks of usable flight height** under the vault's crown. The Wither rises toward flying opponents, and
its marked lanes, skulls and star discharge reach the air. Crossing the outer bounds returns you to the entrance.

**It arrives.** When the first fighter steps in, a tear opens high over the dais, and the Wither falls out of it, lands
in a ring of thrown-up floor, and roars at the room before the first chain. It cannot be hurt until it has landed and
roared.

## Three phases

| Phase | Health | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Threefold Hunger** | 100-67% | Mint eyes and star. It pursues and circles, linking bites, claw rakes, beams, skull rain, floor pulses, tethers and its first brood of skeletons in short chains. |
| **Broken Vessel** | 67-33% | A roar throws everyone back; its armoured pauldrons crash to the floor, fractures light through its bones in violet, the channels burn violet, and skeletal flankers climb out at the wall. Charges, slams and bone spires join longer chains. |
| **Critical Mass** | 33-0% | Ember light, white-hot fractures, an exposed star, ember channels. Its pulse rolls twice, and every third chain is Event Horizon. |

In the arena it stands about **ten blocks tall**: a skeletal sovereign with three heads on stretching necks, long clawed
arms, a hinged ribcage round the star and a dragging tail. Its whole body reads every move before it lands, and every
hit is also marked on the floor or in the air at its real size. The mark is fixed when the windup starts: step off it.

| Move | Its body | The mark | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Maw Snap** | The center head coils back and down | A short lane on the floor with two fangs closing at its end | Step sideways out of the lane |
| **Rending Claw** | One arm cocks across its chest | A wide floor crescent, a white edge sweeping across it the way the arm will | Get behind it or out of the crescent |
| **Threefold Verdict** | All three mouths open, the chest draws back | Three ringed lanes through the air ending at the first stone; side heads take other fighters | Leave the lanes, or put stone between you and the mouths; the beams burn for half a second |
| **Hollow Rain** | Side heads spit upward | Circles that close on you and two spots beside you, a light falling toward each | Move through the gaps; Wind Arms can send a skull back |
| **Starved Pulse** | It hunches and its ribs flare | Rings converge on its feet, then a ring of real floor blocks rolls outward | Jump as the ring reaches you |
| **Grave Tether** | The center head and an arm reach out | A narrow ringed lane with a hook at its end | Leave the lane or break sight; it reaches climbers and fliers |
| **Ribbreaker** | It braces, ribs folded like a prow | A wide lane with running chevrons; **a burst glyph where the lane meets stone** | Step aside. If it hits a pillar or the wall it crashes and is stunned for 2.5 s |
| **Ossuary Slam** | It rises nine blocks, both fists overhead | A broad circle with spokes | Leave the circle, then jump its rolling aftershock |
| **Bone Spires** | Claws dig into the floor | A crack runs to each fighter, glowing hotter | Step off the crack: spires burst along it in order |
| **Bone Brood** | It grips its own ribcage and heaves; the center head rears and retches | A bone-white circle with two lit eyes beside each fighter, and the arc that will land there | Step off the circle; then deal with the wither skeletons it threw |
| **Event Horizon** | Its cage opens wide; it drinks the room toward it for three seconds | Rays from the star across the floor, each stopping at stone: the shadows are safe | Hide behind a pillar, or break the feeding anchor |

Side heads sometimes prepare a separate skull during another move: a mouth light and a fixed aiming thread warn first.
Its attention rotates between fighters in a party.

**Its brood.** Every so often it spews wither skeletons out of its mouth: two at a time in the first phase, three
later, one more for every two extra fighters. Each flies a smoking arc onto its marked circle beside a fighter, hurts
anyone standing there, and fights. No more than three plus one per fighter stand at once (eight at most), and it waits
30, 22 and then 18 seconds between broods. They are its own bones: they crumble to dust after thirty seconds or when it
dies, drop nothing and give no experience, so there is nothing to farm. The skeletons that climb out at the wall when
the vessel breaks are the same brood.

## How hard it is

It is built to be won by reading it, not by tanking it (the bar is Cataclysm's bosses and Bosses'Rise's Souls-like
ones). It has **720 health** (more for a party), and no more than 3.5% of it can be taken in any second, so even a
perfect party needs about forty seconds of clean damage.

| What | First phase | Broken Vessel | Critical Mass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Windups | as listed | about 14% faster | about 26% faster |
| Rest between moves | 70% of the move's | half | a third |
| Slump after a chain | 1.7 s | 1.2 s | 0.8 s |
| Side-head skulls | every 4 s | every 3 s | every 2.25 s |
| Damage (before armour) | bite 11, rake 13, beam 9, skull 8, floor ring 9, hook 7, charge 14, slam 16, spire 9, brood landing 6, discharge 22 | +15% | +30% |

Every hit also withers you (Wither II for four seconds). **Feints:** from the second phase every third bite, rake,
slam and charge holds its coil half again as long. The mark still fills exactly as the hit lands, so dodge when the mark
completes, not when the body moves. **Combos:** from the second phase, whoever Grave Tether hauls in is bitten as they
land; in Critical Mass a charge that misses wheels round and comes again at once (bait the second into stone too).
Its last ring pulses twice in the second phase and three times in the third. **Starving:** in its last 15% it winds up
about 12% faster again, rests less and hits 10% harder.

Its bar is its own: a bone shaft with rib teeth and knuckled ends, its three skulls over the middle, the fill and their
eyes burning mint, violet or ember with the phase, and a pale trail showing the health just lost.

## Turn its hunger against it

| Window | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Rhythm** | After a chain it slumps with its chest open and takes **35% more** damage, briefly (see above). Slams leave it pulling its fists from the floor. |
| **Skill** | **Three spaced heavy hits** during its windups crack its stance (three marks round the star) and break the chain. Fully charged melee strikes, critical arrows and single hits of at least eight count. |
| **Room** | Bait **Ribbreaker** into a pillar: the warning shows a burst where the lane meets stone; it crashes, is stunned for 2.5 s and takes 35% more. When it winds up a heavy move, the nearest unused **anchor pylon** lights and a filament drinks toward the star for seven seconds: break the glass in the pylon's open face (or bounce a skull into it) and the hunger backfires: a tenth of its health, its chain cancelled, its core exposed. Each pylon works once per attempt, and a spent pylon silences one side head's beam. |
| **Power** | Superspeed's Wind Arms return skulls; shield powers hold its death blast; see below. |

Phase finishers force the next phase with a long stagger; in the final tenth they can finish the Wither.

| Powers | Their place in the fight |
| --- | --- |
| Superspeed | Redirect skulls with Wind Arms; reach an active anchor quickly |
| Flight, Pyrokinesis, Hydrokinesis | Movement and recovery windows; the rolling floor can be cleared from above |
| Quake, Sonic Resonance, Telekinesis, Density, Matter Assembly | Pressure, damage and breaking an active anchor's glass; bosses cannot be embedded or lifted |
| Invisibility, Telekinesis, Matter Assembly, Cryokinesis, Sonic Resonance | Their shields hold the death blast (as they hold a nuclear one) |
| Cryokinesis | Chill and slow; never freeze the boss solid |
| Telepathy, Empathic Mimicry | Their usual boss-strength effects and the same anchors as everyone |
| Regeneration, Power Amplification | Sustain and support while a teammate counters |
| Electrokinesis, Radiation, Technopathy, Vibe, Shapeshifting, Space-Time | Their normal kits under the boss rules and damage caps; control buys an opening, never skips the encounter |

## Its death

The killing blow does not end it. It thrashes, light cracking across its bones; its ribs burst outward in a spray of
bone; it rises over the dais with its arms thrown wide and its heads screaming at the vault while the star swells and
beams break out through every fracture. Then the star drinks everything back in, smoke and light spiralling into it,
the room pulled toward it, and goes off.

| The blast | |
| --- | --- |
| **No shield** | Thrown off your feet and flat on your back. A white flash, out cold for two and a half seconds with your ears ringing and the world muffled, then your eyes blink open on the vault before you can get up, about seven and a half seconds in all. |
| **A shield raised toward it** | You slide back, still standing. |
| **A shield-making power** (Invisibility, Telekinesis, Matter Assembly, Cryokinesis, Sonic Resonance) | Your shield flares and holds; you stay standing. |

The blast does no damage: it ends the fight, it doesn't end you. While everyone is still on the ground, the air tears
open **where it died**: the way out, a breach like the one at home, and its prizes are thrown out of it toward the
fighters who earned them. Walk into it to go home (anything of yours still on the floor goes with you). The Ossuary
closes once everyone has left, or two minutes after the victory, sending anyone left home with their prizes; the breach
at the summoning site slams shut.

**Prizes.** A Nether Star for the killing blow, a Fractured Star for every participant, containment notes pointing
toward the powered End portal for first-time winners. A Fractured Star can be crafted into a Breach Seed for another
attempt. Later victories have a one-in-ten chance of the theme's record.

## Music: The Hungry Cage

An original score in E minor at 100 bpm, with a Phrygian F always pulling at the E (the hunger is a half step). Its
heartbeat is three big drum hits and a gap, one for each head, and its kit is bone rattles and chain clanks; its horn
call climbs to a half step at the top and falls home. **Threefold Hunger** is the heartbeat, a growling staccato bass,
low strings and the call in the low horns; **Broken Vessel** adds the full kit, a spiccato ostinato, horns, stabs and a
low choir; **Critical Mass** doubles the drums, puts the call in the high brass and the choir, and adds a high string
tremolo and rising sub at each turn. Phase changes play a stinger and cross to the next loop on the next bar. When it
dies the fight's music stops; after the detonation its last chord rises out of the ringing: the heartbeat failing, a
bell, E minor opening to a bare fifth. It follows the Music slider and is heard only inside the Ossuary. The Gemini:
Heroes Reborn library was a reference for length, loudness, spectrum and density; none of its recordings or melodies is
in the mod.

## Guide and admin

The in-game Guide's **The Hungry Cage** topic covers the breach, reading its body, its windows, the anchors, the death
blast and the way out. Operators can select **Wither: Containment Failure** in the admin console's Story tab to open a
breach near the selected player, enter an eligible player, advance the live phase, **finish it** (straight to the death
scene, the detonation and the exit) or cancel the encounter. Live status names the current attack, stance, health,
fighters, the death scene and the exit's countdown. Phase changes, finishing and cancellation affect the whole
encounter; cancellation returns fighters and also stops a queued formation.
