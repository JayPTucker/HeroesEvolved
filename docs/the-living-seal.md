# The Living Seal and Inheritance

The End is the mod's largest encounter: the native Dragon over its main island, a false victory, then Sylar carrying
the Dragon's energy. Six phases share the island and its airspace. The central island stays where it is; player
builds are left alone. All dialogue, effects, music and new artwork are original.

## The idea

**Power held in orbit, then pulled into the wrong hands.** The Dragon's warnings are violet orbits and white fracture
lines. When Sylar takes them, his brass schematics close around those same shapes. Dragon colours: violet `A78AF2`,
pale cyan `A6F3E8`, bone white `F0F2DC`; Sylar adds brass `E9BC6A` and a dangerous coral `FF8F87` in the last phase.

The powered stronghold portal and the Wither's containment notes lead here. Entry to the encounter requires an
awakened ability and **Power Level 16**. Solo play is fully supported; friends can join late. No power is mandatory.

Sylar is drawn across a fractured worldline. If you defeated him in the Collection, that victory still happened.
He remembers a different possibility. The finale must not undo choices about Claire or Ted in the earlier story.

## The island

The fight uses the actual End main island, its obsidian towers and the Dragon's native multipart body. Its playable
area reaches roughly **240 blocks across**, with flight up to **96 blocks over the central ground**. The original
spikes and the Dragon's full wingspan have room. Permanent player structures are never cleared to make this arena.

Four temporary **grounding altars** sit around the island. A blueprint gives each a rooted, stepped foundation,
recessed light, a glass focus and broken shelter columns. A blueprint refuge near the arrival side provides a way
out and a rally point. Scenery is placed only over air and natural End terrain with no block entities, saved before
placement, and restored on cleanup only where the encounter's own blocks remain. If a footprint is occupied, the
focus remains visible and usable without replacing that build.

While the Dragon lives, each altar's focus holds a Seal Knot (below). Once Sylar takes its power, the altars
become counter-resonators: stand on an inner plate, face the focus and hold an ability key. Any awakened power can
channel. Release or move to stop; held keys never fire a second ability when the channel finishes. A lone player
can overload it; partners can help. The charge drains stamina, with its numbers generated by `tools/balance.py`.

Four visible updrafts let any player take height: hold sneak inside a cyan updraft ring to rise, then release to leave
it. Landing ledges and the island remain valid ways to fight on foot. Low pressure waves follow the island's terrain;
an ordinary, well-timed jump clears them, including on slopes.
The refuge is outside boss targeting and cannot be used to damage a boss. Falling below the island during a live
attempt recalls a participant to the refuge. Leaving the encounter or a restart never takes a player's abilities.

## The Dragon: The Living Seal

**The idea: the seal closing.** The Dragon is the End's power held in orbit around one body. Three violet rings
orbit its chest; that is the Seal, and every attack it makes is the Seal closing on a mark. Each warning is an orbit
of violet rings that converges and seats on the ground exactly as the hit lands, and each weakness you open is a white
fracture across the Seal. Palette: seal violet `A78AF2`, room cyan `A6F3E8` (updrafts, knots, keys), fracture white
`F0F2DC`, live-strike ember `FF5A6E`, and void `1B1030` for shadow.

It is fought the way the bosses skill asks since November 2026: **won by reading it and by using your power, never by
tanking it or by a sword alone.** Every phase is built from locks (`combat/BossLocks`). Each lock has several power
answers that feel different, and one slow answer anyone has. Every one of the twenty powers has a strong answer in
every phase. Every move is animated on the Dragon's own body in five readable beats: an opening pose, a signal, a fast
strike, a held end pose, then the window.

### The room

The four grounding altars around the island each hold a **Seal Knot** at their focus: a knot of the Seal, a violet core
in turning rings, fed by a beam from the nearest tower crystal. Knots are creatures, not blocks, so every power that
hits creatures can hit them, and aimed powers can answer them. Plain weapons do half to a knot; a power's blows do it
all; a power whose key is its blows does more. Breaking a tower crystal (an arrow, a blow, anything) cuts the beam
that feeds its altar's knot, and a knot with no beam never re-forms. The four cyan updrafts are the slow way up.

### Moves

Times are at the first phase. From the second phase every third Bite, Tail Sweep, Seal Rush, Riftdive and Rift Leap
is a feint that holds its opening pose half again as long: dodge when the mark fills, not when the body moves. Moves
come faster each phase (86%, then 74%), never under 0.4 s for the bite, 0.5 s for the harpoon and 0.6 s for anything
with an area mark. It picks a chain of two to four moves by where its focus stands (close, mid, far, high), never by
dice, and slumps after each chain with its guard open.

### Weak windows

| Window | What opens it |
| --- | --- |
| **Skill** | Three charged hits (a full swing, a critical arrow or any blow of 8 or more), spaced at least 0.4 s apart, during its windups break its stance: the chain is gone and it reels for 1.8 s. Its Descent, Take-off and The Unmaking can't be broken. |
| **Rhythm** | After every chain it slumps for 1.5 s (1.1, then 0.8 s in later phases) taking 35% more. On the ground it takes full damage and its head 120%. After The Unmaking it lands exhausted. |

Its native contact hits are gone: the body hurts only through its warned moves. Its body follows a planned path for
every committed move and turns its lanes so no dive or run passes through a tower.

### Numbers

It is tuned for Power Level 16 and scales to who is fighting it, not just how many: each fighter counts 1 with an
awakened power at level 16, 0.06 more or less for each level above or below (never under 0.6), and 0.5 without one.
Its health is 800 × (1 + 0.5 × (strength − 1)), from 70% to three times, raised as people join or level up and never
lowered. Its blows run from a fifth softer against weaker parties to a tenth harder against stronger ones, and its
warnings run up to 15% longer against weaker ones. No blow takes more than 5% of its health and no second more than
2.5%, so even a flawless fight lasts about forty seconds whatever the weapon. Its blows run 6 to 22 before armour,
15% harder each phase; 30% of every blow is the Seal's fracture, which no armour stops, and its heavy blows also take
5% of your maximum health.

## The false victory

The Dragon still falls through its native death animation. Its experience light is caught instead of awarded early.
The apparent victory chord hangs. Then the light bends sideways. A worldline breach opens, Sylar steps out, raises one
hand, and the Dragon's dying streams enter his chest. Violet ribs of light become the frame of a pair of vast wings.
The Dragon's motif returns inside Sylar's theme. Story lines go to chat, one beat at a time.

The ordinary exit, egg and gateway completion wait for the full encounter. The refuge still offers retreat. Reaching
Sylar records a checkpoint: a wipe, retreat or restart during his battle starts a fresh Sylar attempt, rather than
making the party kill the Dragon again. The Dragon's experience is saved and pays once when the finale is won.

## Sylar: Inheritance

The existing Sylar entity gains a separate endgame mode; his Collection fight retains its own rules and history.
Here he fights across the island, combines Dragon geometry with familiar power gestures, and reads the gifts brought
against him. His voice addresses each of the twenty powers. The first glimpse is a human silhouette inside a
Dragon-sized web of energy, not an enlarged ordinary zombie.

| **One More Future** | 33–0% | His body cannot contain the star. The wing web sheds shards, the altars flare, and a final broad signature has a readable safe answer. |

Prisms are objectives, not an excuse to disable a player's kit. Each broken prism returns a large bite through his
ordinary caps, ends its cast and gives a stagger. Finishers work at phase thresholds and in his final tenth.

Large discharges give every fighter a golden safe orbit seven blocks from where they stood when the warning began.
These shelters have a 3.5-block radius and work at flight height too. The sector and orbits remain fixed throughout
the warning; standing still in the original marked position is not automatically safe.

## The light breaks

Sylar's final death lasts eight seconds. He arches back, his arms spread and white fractures widen across his actual
face, coat and limbs. His chest splits into light-edged fragments. Rays of the twenty gifts pour out through the
cracks, growing into a brilliant white core.

At 3.6 seconds his silhouette breaks apart. A huge three-dimensional energy wave races across and beyond the island,
with lightning, crystalline shards, pressure rings and colored filaments woven into the front. Rolling translucent
billows and a rising crown use the prologue explosion's volumetric technique; the released abilities replace its
fire and smoke. The light disperses, then the saved experience, rewards and native exit are released.

The victory wave leaves players and their builds intact. The existing reduced-flashing option softens its flash.

## Rules and outcomes

The Dragon's numbers are under "Numbers" above (800 for one fighter of strength 1, scaled by the party's strength, caps of
5% a blow and 2.5% a second). Inheritance Sylar has 800 for one fighter; additional participants add 50%, capped at
three times solo. For both, late joins preserve the health fraction and leaving never lowers the pool, and effective
health stays correct beyond the vanilla maximum-health attribute limit. Sylar's ordinary damage has a 6% per-hit and 4%
rolling second cap; the first hit in an empty window may use its per-hit cap. Backfires, returned shards and phase
finishers are exempt.
Full ranged damage reaches 96 blocks and fades by 160. Non-player blows chip at most 3 effective health. Native
terrain explosions, falls, drowning, suffocation and portal exploits cannot bypass the fight. Idle bosses recover
after ten quiet seconds. Twenty seconds with no fighters resets the current checkpoint's attempt.

| Outcome | What happens | Return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full victory | Sylar fractures and explodes in an island-wide ability wave, saved Dragon XP pays once, the native exit/egg/gateway finish, and participant rewards are delivered | Normal End exploration and a crafted re-fight |
| All down or retreat during Dragon | Belongings are saved for respawn; the Dragon attempt resets | A fresh Dragon attempt |
| All down or retreat after absorption | Belongings are returned; the Sylar checkpoint remains | A fresh Sylar attempt |
| Restart | Temporary scenery is restored, powers and belongings are preserved, checkpoint state survives | Resume the correct stage with a fresh boss attempt |
| Player joins late | Eligible fighters join the live attempt; the pool grows without healing its fraction | No lockout |

Operators have an **End: The Living Seal** entry in the admin menu: live status (phase, health and party scaling, the
knots, whether it flies, its move and stance), and controls to wake the Dragon for the selected player (bringing them to
the main island and summoning a fresh Dragon if the last one died), advance a phase, break its locks (the Seal, or a
feeding knot's backfire), ground it, finish the Dragon (straight to Sylar), or cancel the attempt.

Every participant earns an **Inheritance Core**, which crafts a **Worldline Sigil** for a re-fight. First-time winners
also earn an **Anchor of Return**: hold it in either hand to survive one fall into the End void and return to safety.
That protection opens safer exploration beyond the main island. Repeat wins can drop a record of the finale's theme.
The vanilla four-crystal Dragon respawn remains supported.

## Music and verification

Six original 100 BPM phase loops share a tempo and key, with a nine-second false-victory/absorption cue, phase stingers
and a nine-second rupture cue timed to the detonation. The Gemini: Heroes Reborn Gameplay Score states, transitions and short cues are structural and instrumental
references. No recording or melody from that library is shipped. Each loop is measured for loudness, clipping and
seams; all music follows the Music slider.

Tests cover native multipart damage, every power's strong answer in every phase, the Seal's gate and its knots, the
crystals that feed them, aimed bring-downs, the feeding knot's backfire, Riftbrand, the open star, The Unmaking's
power answers, feints and every windup's floor, strength scaling and armour-piercing blows, the admin controls,
counter-resonator charging and cancellation, Sylar's shapes, party scaling beyond the attribute limit, all twenty
patterns, checkpoint recovery, preserved player blocks, death drops, vanilla progression and reward duplication. A real client preview photographs every warning,
phase, the absorption, aerial combat, prism backfire and the ending.

Visual references for the ruins include Mojang's [End city overview](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/end-city)
and the builder's [Epic World: The End](https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/epic-world-the-end-minecraft-1-9x-1-12x/).
These inform tall silhouettes, pale stone against obsidian and readable paths. No schematic, texture or world from
